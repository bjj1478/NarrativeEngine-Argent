# Argent — design goals

A fork of Narrative Engine tuned for solo play. The engine decides *when* something is
uncertain and states the terms; the player rolls the dice and does the maths; the prose
mentions neither.

## Goals

**1. Mechanics stay off the page.**
No subsystem is ever named in narration — no attribute, tier, die, threshold or bonus.
Outcomes read as cause in the world ("hinges recently oiled"), never as machinery
("your Perception, which has been useless all day").

**2. The GM asks; the player rolls.**
When an action needs dice the GM states the die, the reason, the bar to beat and what a
miss costs — then stops and waits. The bar is committed to before the number exists, so
it cannot be bent to suit the result. The player reports one total, bonuses included.

**3. Pacing follows the stakes.**
Tense scenes run short and hand the turn back the moment a decision lands. Calm scenes
may breathe. No fixed beat quota, in either direction.

**4. Prose is lean.**
Specific detail over accumulated detail. A sentence that adds no new fact is cut.

**5. Consequences live in the world, not the engine.**
What success, failure and consequences *mean* belongs to the ruleset and the world docs,
per genre. The engine only needs to know the outcome of a check/roll.

**6. Numbers never reach the writer.**
Anything the engine computes is surfaced as words, or withheld. A number in the prompt is
a number the writer can read back out.

## Non-goals

* A numeric character sheet, derived stats, or a combat maths layer.
* The engine rolling for the player, beyond an explicit opt-in fallback.
* Uniform, predictable turns. Variable pacing is the point.
